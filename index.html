Multimedia & Authoring

What is Multimedia?

Multimedia is the combination of different media elements such as text, images, audio, video, and animation to deliver information in an engaging way.

It is widely used in websites, games, films, and educational platforms to improve user experience and communication.

Video Editing

Video editing is the post-production and arrangement of video shots. Video editors draw from a variety of techniques from film cinematography, television, and other sorts of videography. Video editing structures and presents all video information, including films and television shows, video advertisements and video essays. Video editing has been dramatically democratized in recent years by editing software available for personal computers. Editing video can be difficult and tedious, so several technologies have been produced to aid people in this task. Overall, video editing has a wide variety of styles and applications.

Audio Editing

Audio editing involves manipulating recorded audio to achieve a desired outcome, whether for music, podcasts, film, or other multimedia projects. The process can include cutting, trimming, splicing, rearranging, and combining audio clips, as well as adjusting volume, pitch, and tempo to create a polished final product. It is distinct from mixing or mastering, which focus on balancing and finalizing the overall sound rather than cleaning or structuring individual tracks.

Graphic Design

Graphic design is a profession, academic discipline and applied art that involves creating visual communications intended to transmit specific messages to social groups, with specific objectives. Graphic design is an interdisciplinary branch of design and of the fine arts. Its practice involves creativity, innovation and lateral thinking using manual or digital tools, where it is usual to use text and graphics to communicate visually.

Animation

Animation is a method of photographing successive drawings, models, or even puppets, to create an illusion of movement in a sequence. Because our eyes can only retain an image for approximately 1/10 of a second, when multiple images appear in fast succession, the brain blends them into a single moving image. In traditional animation, pictures are drawn or painted on transparent celluloid sheets to be photographed. Early cartoons are examples of this, but today, most animated movies are made with computer-generated imagery or CGI. To create the appearance of smooth motion from these drawn, painted, or computer-generated images, frame rate, or the number of consecutive images that are displayed each second, is considered. Moving characters are usually shot “on twos” which just means one image is shown for two frames, totaling in at 12 drawings per second. 12 frames per second allows for motion but may look choppy. In the film, a frame rate of 24 frames per second is often used for smooth motion.

Authoring Tools

An authoring tool is a program designed to simplify the creation of structured digital content, including text, graphics, audio, video, quizzes, and interactive elements, often for educational or corporate training purposes. These tools allow users to develop multimedia applications without needing extensive programming knowledge, enabling the integration of various media types into cohesive learning modules or presentations. Authoring tools often include templates, drag-and-drop interfaces, and pre-built components to facilitate the design process.
